The Vale: a world that fades and repaints

Every colour on the screen goes through one table, which greys out each ink you do not hold yet. Win an ink and the table is rebuilt: the room repaints itself column by column, and every room after it is a little brighter. The inks are keys too: BLUE freezes fords, RED burns thorns, GREEN grows vines.

The Spectrum has eight inks. The Vale has lost them all but the grey. Every attribute the game draws, text and border included, goes through FADE, a 256-byte table: an ink whose colour you do not hold shows as grey, a paper as black. Monsters never fade. They are made of stolen ink.

How it works

The repaint

Winning an ink rebuilds the table and then the room you are standing in, column by column across the screen, so you watch the colour come back. A room that has been painted with a spell remembers it: frozen fords stay frozen, burnt thorns stay gone.

Colours as keys

Gates are tiles. Walk into shallow water with BLUE and it freezes. Walk into a thorn hedge with RED and it burns. Walk into the Rift with GREEN and vines grow across. A great white door wants every colour at once. There is a cave in the Grey Peaks too, as dark as a shut eye.

Walking without flicker

A room is 16 by 10 tiles of 16 by 16 pixels. The hero and the people move two pixels a frame through a three-by-three cell window. Each frame it is built in memory with every person masked over the tiles, then copied to the screen straight after the interrupt, so nothing flickers. The hero's picture is stored shifted four ways. The water's glint changes only attributes, and only for the tiles whose glint moves.

How it was checked

Every room is drawn by the build into the two maps on this page, in full colour, and looked at. The checks walk through a gate of each kind with and without its ink, repaint a room at an altar, and time a walk across the Mere, the busiest room, to catch a lost frame. A search of the world shows how much of it each ink opens, and that the game can still be finished.

Load it yourself

For the 128K. Two tapes of the same game. inkwright.tap plays the title tune while it loads, and needs an emulator that plays the tape in real time (ZX Spin, Retro Virtual Machine). inkwright-plain.tap loads silently through the ROM, for emulators that load tapes in a flash.

  1. It needs a 128K (a 128K or a grey +2). Pick Tape Loader. On a 48K the tape says it needs a 128K and stops.
  2. The singing tape, inkwright.tap, plays the title tune while it loads. Its own loader reads the tape, so the emulator must play it in real time: ZX Spin and Retro Virtual Machine do. Switch off instant and fast loading. The title comes up after about five minutes.
  3. The plain tape, inkwright-plain.tap, is the same game loaded silently by the ROM, for emulators that load tapes in a flash.
  4. Saving: at an inn, start the emulator's tape recording, then say yes. To carry on later, load that tape with Load from tape on the title.
  5. On a real Spectrum: play the tape into the EAR socket from a tape player or a TZX player.
