Fights: the border loads each monster

A fight does not cut to its screen: it loads it, like a tape. Pilot tone in red and cyan, a header, then the picture byte by byte in the Spectrum's own screen order, blue and yellow stripes in the border and the screech of the data with it. Then the colour wheel decides it: a monster hit with its complement takes three times the damage.

Fights are turn by turn: FIGHT, INK, ITEM, FLEE. The screen for each is drawn out of sight on the 128K's second screen, then loaded onto the first as a tape would load it. The ending loads one last loading screen the same way.

How it works

Complements

Two Spectrum colours are complements when their numbers add up to 7, white: BLUE and YELLOW, RED and CYAN, MAGENTA and GREEN. A spell on a monster of its complement does three times the damage. On a monster of its own colour it heals it. The Grey King is black, and takes half from anything but WHITE. The legend under every fight says the pairs, and greys out the colours you do not hold yet.

Inks are mixed as the Spectrum mixes them: BLUE and RED make MAGENTA, BLUE and GREEN make CYAN, RED and GREEN make YELLOW. Each is a new spell to cast once you hold both halves.

The monsters

Twenty-one kinds of monster from sixteen pictures of 48 by 48 pixels, some of them painted in other inks, then three shrine guardians and the Grey King. A monster smeared with FLASH can curse you, and while your HP flashes half your strokes miss. Every monster you meet goes in the colour journal in its own colour.

The loader

It is the ROM's loader turned into a show. The pilot edges are 2,168 T-states apart, as the ROM's are. The data is blue and yellow, a stripe an edge, the edges as long as the ROM's zeros and ones, and the speaker clicks with every edge, so a monster sounds like a loading screen. The picture lands in the screen's own order, thirds and lines, and its colours come last.

How it was checked

The build walks into a fight and takes six screenshots of its load, three of them on this page. Its checks cast a complement and see three times the damage, cast a monster's own colour and see it healed, beat a guardian, and fight the Grey King with WHITE to the ending. A balance simulation plays the fights level by level. The numbers are first guesses, still to be tuned by playing.

Download

Load it yourself

For the 128K. Three tapes, one game. inkwright-turbo.tzx lets you make your apprentice while it loads, in 3 minutes; inkwright-special.tap does the same at the ROM's own speed, in 6. Both must play in real time, as a cassette does (a real 128K, or ZX Spin or Retro Virtual Machine with fast loading off). inkwright-normal.tap loads the ordinary way and works in any emulator, even ones that load instantly; the game asks the same questions when you pick NEW JOURNEY. Not sure? Take the normal one.

  1. It needs a 128K (a 128K or a grey +2). Pick Tape Loader. On a 48K the tape says it needs a 128K and stops.
  2. Two tapes, one game: pick by how your emulator loads. Once you are playing they are exactly the same.
  3. The special tape, inkwright-special.tap, has the loading page: while it loads you type your name and choose a keepsake and a cloak, with a candle burning down beside you. The Spectrum's own loader can't do that, so the game reads the tape itself, and the tape has to play in real time, the way a cassette does. Use it on a real 128K, or in ZX Spin or Retro Virtual Machine with instant and fast loading switched off. It takes about six minutes.
  4. The normal tape, inkwright-normal.tap, loads the ordinary way, through the Spectrum's own loader, so it works in every emulator, including the ones that load a tape instantly (Fuse with fast loading, phone and web emulators). There is no loading page: when you pick NEW JOURNEY the game asks the same questions on its own screens. Not sure which? Take this one.
  5. Saving: at an inn, start the emulator's tape recording, then say yes. To carry on later, load that tape with Load from tape on the title.
  6. On a real Spectrum: play the tape into the EAR socket from a tape player or a TZX player.
