The tape that sings

While INKWRIGHT loads, the AY plays its title tune. The tape is an ordinary .tap at ROM speed, but after the first block its own loader reads every edge of the signal, and between edges writes one register of the tune to the sound chip. Melody and bass, the whole five minutes.

A game this size takes about five minutes from tape. This one spends them playing its own title tune on the 128K's AY chip, with the blue and yellow stripes going in the border as usual. It is still a plain .tap at the ROM's speed. Nothing about the tape is special: the music is all in the loader.

How it works

One register an edge

The ROM loads a BASIC line and a small loader. From there the loader reads the tape itself: it times each edge of the signal, as the ROM does, and once an edge it writes one register of the tune's current frame to the AY, the next one each time. The tune is stored as frames of 8 bytes, the periods and volumes of channels A and B, the noise and the mixer, so a frame is written over and over between interrupts. The interrupt only moves it on to the next frame, 50 times a second. The extra work is the same length every time, 196 T-states, and the edge timing allows for it.

Loader Lab's loader

The loader is the one from our Loader Lab, where the same trick first played Shardstorm's tune, used as it is. INKWRIGHT adds the blocks it loads: the loading screen, the code, the people and the banks, packed small and unpacked in place at the end.

Which emulator

An emulator that loads a tape by catching the ROM's loader and copying the bytes in can't run this one, because it is not the ROM reading the tape. Play it in real time in ZX Spin or Retro Virtual Machine, or on a real 128K. For the other kind, the plain tape loads the same game through the ROM, without the tune.

How it was checked

The build loads the singing tape through our emulator's tape deck the way a person would, edge by edge, with no shortcut: our loader takes over 26 seconds in, and the title appears 288 seconds in. The minute on this page was recorded from that load. The plain tape is loaded the same way, and a 48K is shown to say it needs a 128K. Not yet heard from a real tape on a real 128K.

Load it yourself

For the 128K. Two tapes of the same game. inkwright.tap plays the title tune while it loads, and needs an emulator that plays the tape in real time (ZX Spin, Retro Virtual Machine). inkwright-plain.tap loads silently through the ROM, for emulators that load tapes in a flash.

  1. It needs a 128K (a 128K or a grey +2). Pick Tape Loader. On a 48K the tape says it needs a 128K and stops.
  2. The singing tape, inkwright.tap, plays the title tune while it loads. Its own loader reads the tape, so the emulator must play it in real time: ZX Spin and Retro Virtual Machine do. Switch off instant and fast loading. The title comes up after about five minutes.
  3. The plain tape, inkwright-plain.tap, is the same game loaded silently by the ROM, for emulators that load tapes in a flash.
  4. Saving: at an inn, start the emulator's tape recording, then say yes. To carry on later, load that tape with Load from tape on the title.
  5. On a real Spectrum: play the tape into the EAR socket from a tape player or a TZX player.
